  - per-alert Wilcoxon tests

- **Formalism**: numbered equations, a production-rule base R1–R3, the derived switch points (9/32, 23/32) and a Bayes reference rule (Eq. 7). (Algorithm 1 was removed in the page-limit trim; Eqs. (1)–(7) and R1–R3 define the method, and its data-split step now sits in Section 3.)
- **Honest negatives now in the paper**:
  - GB is the best detector in 10/10 seeds.
  - Fuzzy and crisp bands give identical actions, so the gain comes from the Soft-block band.
  - The Bayes rule is much cheaper (2.164).
  - Top-20% is cheaper at C_FN ≤ 3.
  - Seed 48 is weak.
  - A test-oracle threshold ≤ 0.10 would beat the rules.
- **Business/DBA angle**: SOC-manager framing, digital transformation, and "calibrate the cost matrix before automating" as a managerial finding.
- **Limitations**: Section 5 ("Limitations") covers failure modes, data age and scope, the illustrative costs and the absence of a deployment, drift analysis or user study; Section 6 is the Conclusion.
- **Rounding fix**: the old Markdown said the fuzzy cost was 3.069. The value in `summary.json` is 3.06755, which rounds to 3.068, the value used everywhere now.
