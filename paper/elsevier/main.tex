\begin{abstract}
Security operations centres routinely obtain high-quality alert scores from machine learning, yet the mapping from score to operational action is often left to brittle thresholds. This short communication proposes a fuzzy--neural decision intelligence framework that treats cyber alert triage as a knowledge-based decision problem. A compact multilayer perceptron estimates attack propensity from NSL-KDD features. Triangular fuzzy membership functions convert propensity into linguistic Low / Medium / High risk bands. A cost-aware decision layer maps those bands to Monitor, Soft-block, or Escalate actions under an explicit relative cost model. In a reproducible CPU-scale experiment, gradient boosting attains the highest ROC-AUC (0.947). The fuzzy--neural policy, using the neural propensity scores, reduces mean expected triage cost to 3.069 versus 3.264 for a hard 0.5 threshold and 4.309 for a top-20\% escalate rule, while raising attack coverage to 0.686. The claimed contribution is decision-facing knowledge representation and cost-aware actioning, not a new state-of-the-art detector.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Artificial intelligence and knowledge-based methods are widely used to support prediction and human decision-making in security operations. For intrusion alerts, analysts must decide which events to monitor, soft-contain, or escalate under limited response capacity.

Accuracy-centric detectors optimise ROC-AUC or F1 and stop at a score. Pure expert rule bases are transparent but brittle. Fuzzy--neural hybrids retain learning capacity while exposing linguistic grades operators can inspect, aligning with the aims of \emph{Knowledge-Based Systems} for intelligent decision support and computational intelligence.

\paragraph{Research gap.}
Prior fuzzy and neural IDS work largely improves detection rates. Fewer studies close the loop from learned propensity to linguistic knowledge bands to cost-aware triage actions, evaluated with decision metrics rather than classifier metrics alone.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A three-layer fuzzy--neural decision pipeline for cyber alert triage.
(2) An evaluation protocol reporting decision quality alongside predictive metrics on public NSL-KDD data.
(3) A reproducible short-cycle CPU experiment with open artifacts.

\section{Related work}
Machine learning for intrusion detection on NSL-KDD and related corpora is extensively surveyed~\cite{buczak2016,ring2019,tavallaee2009}. Fuzzy and neuro-fuzzy IDS designs improve detection and interpretability~\cite{jang1993,srilatha2021,liu2022plos,liu2022srep}. Cost-sensitive learning formalises asymmetric errors~\cite{elkan2001}. Security analytics emphasises prioritisation and incident prediction~\cite{sun2019,algaradi2020}. Explainability methods such as SHAP can aid trust~\cite{lundberg2017}, but explanation alone does not select Monitor / Soft-block / Escalate under budget. We claim a compact knowledge-based decision layer evaluated with cost and coverage, not a new highest AUC.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Problem formulation}
For features $x_i$ and label $y_i\in\{0,1\}$, the system outputs propensity $p_i=f_\theta(x_i)\in[0,1]$, fuzzy memberships $\mu_L,\mu_M,\mu_H$, and action $a_i\in\{\mathrm{Monitor},\mathrm{SoftBlock},\mathrm{Escalate}\}$.

\subsection{Neural propensity model}
An MLP (128--64--1) with ReLU, dropout, and class-weighted BCE estimates $p_i$. Numeric features are standardised; protocol, service, and flag are one-hot encoded~\cite{goodfellow2016}.

\subsection{Fuzzy risk banding}
Triangular memberships on $p$ define Low / Medium / High risk~\cite{zadeh1965}. Dominant membership yields an inspectable linguistic band.

\subsection{Cost-aware decision layer}
Relative costs penalise missed attacks and unnecessary escalations. Dominant Low$\rightarrow$Monitor, Medium$\rightarrow$SoftBlock, High$\rightarrow$Escalate. Baselines: hard threshold $0.5$; escalate top $20\%$ by score.

\section{Experimental setup}
NSL-KDD train/test~\cite{tavallaee2009}, binary normal vs attack. Training subsampled to 40{,}000 rows; test capped at 20{,}000 (seed 42). Baselines: logistic regression; gradient boosting~\cite{chen2016}; neural ablation; proposed fuzzy--neural policy. Metrics: Accuracy, F1, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC; mean expected cost; attack coverage; unnecessary escalations. CPU-only Python/PyTorch run well under two hours.

\section{Discussion}
The design combines learning, fuzzy knowledge presentation, and decision support for prediction/warning settings. We do not claim state-of-the-art NSL-KDD detection; pairing the fuzzy--cost layer with stronger propensity models is a natural extension. Limitations include public benchmark traffic, scenario-default costs, and training subsample for speed.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a fuzzy--neural decision intelligence framework for cost-aware cyber alert triage. Reproducible NSL-KDD results show improved decision cost and coverage under linguistic Monitor / Soft-block / Escalate actions. Future work includes ANFIS-style adaptive memberships, operator-calibrated costs, and phishing-transfer evaluation.
